Soap Making Fundamentals

Soap production requires understanding saponification—the chemical reaction between fats/oils and an alkaline substance. Three primary methods exist: cold process, hot process, and rendered tallow with wood ash lye.

Cold Process Soap Making

The cold process method produces high-quality soap while minimizing heat requirements and ingredient degradation. This method works best in temperate climates and requires careful measurement and safety protocols.

Safety First: Lye (sodium hydroxide or potassium hydroxide) is caustic and burns skin and eyes. Always wear chemical-resistant gloves, eye protection, and long sleeves. Work in ventilated areas. Have vinegar available to neutralize accidental splashes.

Basic Cold Process Recipe:

IngredientWeight/VolumeNotes
Animal fat (tallow/lard)600gRendered and cleaned
Coconut oil (if available)200gCreates lather; optional
Lye (sodium hydroxide)85gFrom wood ash or purchased
Water200mlDistilled or filtered preferred
Essential oils/botanicalsOptionalFor fragrance and properties

Step-by-step process:

  1. Weigh all ingredients precisely using balance scale or graduated containers with water displacement method
  2. Dissolve lye in cold water in heat-resistant glass or stainless steel container (add lye to water, never water to lye). Solution heats naturally
  3. Heat fats to 38-48°C (100-120°F) using water bath method
  4. When both lye solution and fat reach 38-40°C, slowly pour lye solution into fat while stirring continuously
  5. Continue stirring for 15-30 minutes until mixture reaches "trace"—the consistency of thin pudding where drizzled mixture momentarily sits on surface
  6. Add essential oils or botanicals once trace is reached
  7. Pour into molds lined with cloth and allow 24-48 hours to gel
  8. Cut into bars and cure for 4-6 weeks, turning daily for air circulation

Hot Process Soap Making

Hot process soap accelerates saponification using heat, producing usable soap within 24 hours. This method is preferable when immediate soap production is necessary and provides more control in variable conditions.

Hot Process Method:

  1. Combine lye solution and fats as in cold process, stirring to trace
  2. Transfer mixture to heavy pot or iron cauldron over low heat
  3. Stir frequently as heat causes saponification to progress (mixture will separate, heat will darken color slightly)
  4. Process continues 30-90 minutes until mixture becomes translucent, thick paste-like consistency
  5. Test completion: small amount on glass plate should solidify within seconds
  6. Cool slightly, add botanicals or fragrance, then pour into molds
  7. Soap hardens within 24 hours and can be used immediately, though curing for 2-3 weeks improves quality

Lye Production from Wood Ash

When commercial lye is unavailable, extract lye from wood ash. This process requires patience and produces variable-strength lye, necessitating careful testing.

Wood Ash Lye Extraction:

  1. Collect ash from hardwood fires (oak, maple, ash preferred; avoid softwoods which create gummy residue)
  2. Fill large container with ash, then pour water slowly until saturated. Ash absorbs water completely
  3. Allow to settle 24-48 hours as lye leaches into water
  4. Carefully pour off liquid (lye solution) into separate container, leaving settled ash debris
  5. Filter through cloth multiple times to clarify
  6. Test lye strength: immerse raw potato in solution. If potato skin darkens or dissolves, lye is ready; if unaffected, evaporate solution to concentrate by boiling over fire
  7. Lye from ash is potassium hydroxide (softer than sodium hydroxide), producing softer "liquid soap" better suited for washing and shampoo than hard bar soap

Toothpaste and Tooth Powder Production

Simple Tooth Powder

Tooth powder is easier to produce than toothpaste and equally effective. Traditional formulations combine abrasive minerals with antimicrobial herbs.

Basic Recipe:

IngredientQuantityFunction
Chalk or finely ground bone4 tablespoonsAbrasive cleaning agent
Peppermint or spearmint powder2 tablespoonsFlavor and antimicrobial
Myrrh powder1 tablespoonGum health and antimicrobial
Salt1 tablespoonMineral content, gum stimulation
Essential oil (peppermint)3-5 dropsFlavor and preservation

Mix all ingredients thoroughly in mortar and pestle until completely uniform. Store in sealed clay pot or glass jar. Use small pinch on damp brush, wet brush, and brush teeth as normal.

Herbal Tooth Powder Alternatives

For sensitive teeth: Increase myrrh to 2 tablespoons, add 1 tablespoon dried sage powder, reduce salt to 1 teaspoon

For whitening: Use activated charcoal (1 tablespoon) in place of half the chalk, add 1 tablespoon strawberry powder or dried strawberry

For antimicrobial action: Substitute 1 tablespoon myrrh with clove powder; add 1 tablespoon turmeric powder

Natural Toothpaste Base

For paste rather than powder, create base of coconut oil and calcium compound, adding herbs as needed:

  1. Melt 4 tablespoons coconut oil gently over low heat
  2. Mix with 2 tablespoons finely ground bone or chalk
  3. Add 1 tablespoon peppermint powder and 1 teaspoon myrrh powder
  4. Add 2-3 drops peppermint essential oil
  5. Stir thoroughly, pour into small clay pots before oil solidifies
  6. Cool completely before use

This toothpaste is very soft in warm climates; increase calcium compound to 3 tablespoons for firmer paste.

Shampoo Alternatives

Traditional soaps work well as shampoo but leave residue. Herbal rinses improve results without commercial chemistry.

Soap-Based Shampoo Bar

Formulate soft shampoo bars specifically for hair using rendered fat with antimicrobial botanicals:

  1. Follow hot process soap method using 80% lard, 20% coconut oil
  2. At thick-paste stage, add finely powdered blend of dried nettle (2 tablespoons), dried rosemary (1 tablespoon), dried lavender (1 tablespoon)
  3. Mix thoroughly and pour into molds
  4. Cut and cure for 3 weeks

Herbal Rinses (Essential for Hair Health)

After washing hair with any soap, use herbal rinses to remove residue and improve shine:

HerbPreparationBest For
Apple cider vinegar1 part vinegar to 3 parts waterAll hair types, removes soap buildup
RosemarySimmer 2 cups dried rosemary in 1 liter water 30 min, cool and strainDark hair, stimulates growth, removes dandruff
NettleSteep 1 handful dried nettle in 1 liter hot water 30 min, strainFine/thin hair, strengthening
ChamomileSteep 2 tablespoons dried chamomile in 1 liter hot water 1 hour, strainBlonde/light hair, conditioning
SageBoil 1 handful dried sage in 1 liter water 45 min, strainDark hair, prevents graying, anti-dandruff

Dry Shampoo Alternative

For quick cleaning without water:

  1. Mix 4 tablespoons finely ground oat flour or rice powder with 1 tablespoon dried lavender powder
  2. Optional: add 1 tablespoon powdered orris root for additional fragrance fixation
  3. Store in cloth sachet or perforated clay pot
  4. Sprinkle sparingly onto roots, massage scalp thoroughly, brush or comb hair to remove powder

Natural Deodorant Production

Effective deodorants address both odor bacteria and moisture. Simple formulations provide reliable results.

Powder Deodorant

The simplest, most reliable formula:

IngredientQuantityFunction
Baking soda (sodium bicarbonate)3 tablespoonsNeutralizes odor acids
Arrowroot powder or tapioca starch3 tablespoonsAbsorbs moisture
Essential oil (lavender or cedarwood)5-8 dropsFragrance and antimicrobial
Dried lavender or herbs1 tablespoon finely groundAdditional fragrance

Mix dry ingredients thoroughly, then add essential oil drop by drop while stirring to distribute evenly. Store in sealed clay pot. Apply light dusting with cloth pad or small brush to underarms.

Solid Stick Deodorant

For firm application and less dust:

  1. Combine 3 tablespoons baking soda, 3 tablespoons arrowroot powder, and 4 tablespoons coconut oil in small container
  2. Heat gently over low flame until oil melts, mixing thoroughly
  3. Add 5-8 drops essential oil and stir
  4. Pour into small clay pots before solidifying, or use improvised stick molds (smooth wood or rolled bark)
  5. Allow to harden completely (2-4 hours) before use

Paste Deodorant (Damp Climate Version)

In humid environments where powders cake, a paste works better:

  1. Combine 2 tablespoons baking soda with 2 tablespoons coconut oil and 1 tablespoon shea butter (if available)
  2. Mix until smooth paste, add 4-5 drops essential oil
  3. Store in small sealed pot
  4. Apply small amount to underarms using fingertips
Baking Soda Sensitivity: Some individuals develop irritation from concentrated baking soda. If redness or burning occurs, reduce baking soda to 1-2 tablespoons and increase arrowroot/starch to 4-5 tablespoons. Alternatively, use formula without baking soda (see below).

Aluminum-Free Alternative (No Baking Soda)

For sensitive skin:

  1. Mix 2 tablespoons arrowroot powder with 2 tablespoons finely ground sage powder
  2. Add 2 tablespoons coconut oil and 5 drops cedarwood essential oil
  3. Stir into paste consistency
  4. Apply sparingly to underarms

This formulation is gentler but less effective in very high humidity; reapplication during day may be necessary.

Menstrual Product Manufacturing

Sustainable menstrual products are essential for female health and dignity. Several practical options provide reliable, washable solutions.

Cloth Menstrual Pads

Materials needed:

  • Natural cotton or linen fabric (preferably unbleached)
  • Absorbent inner layers (flannel, cotton batting, or hemp cloth)
  • Waterproof backing (natural plant oils in fabric, or thin bark/skin)
  • Thread (plant fiber or animal sinew)
  • Small fastening strips or buttons

Construction method:

  1. Cut outer fabric to 7-9 inches long, 2-3 inches wide
  2. Cut absorbent inner layer slightly smaller (6-8 inches long, 2 inches wide)
  3. Layer: outer fabric bottom, 3-4 absorbent layers in middle, outer fabric top
  4. Stitch all layers together with small stitches around perimeter
  5. Sew fastening strips (3-4 inches long) to each side for wrapping around undergarment
  6. Optional: create pocket on back side to insert additional absorbent material

Washing and maintenance: Rinse immediately in cold water, soak overnight in water with ash lye or vinegar, then boil for 30 minutes to sterilize. Dry thoroughly in sun (UV kills remaining bacteria). Store in dry location. Expected lifespan: 3-5 years with proper care.

Menstrual Cup Alternative (Wood or Ceramic)

A simple cup shape provides reusable collection without pads:

  1. Carve or throw from ceramic a small cup 4-5cm tall, 3cm diameter
  2. Fire to hardness if ceramic
  3. Smooth all edges thoroughly (critical for comfort)
  4. Create attachment cord (braided plant fiber or sinew, 12-15cm) attached near rim
  5. Boil in water 20 minutes to sterilize before first use

Empty cup several times daily, rinse in water, reinsert. Boil daily or weekly for sterilization depending on infection risk in your community.

Wool Felted Pads

Wool provides natural waterproofing, excellent absorption, and antimicrobial properties:

  1. Wet-felt wool fibers into pad shape (see woolworking techniques) approximately 3mm thick, 3 inches wide, 8 inches long
  2. Felt provides waterproof backing naturally
  3. Sew cotton covering on one side for comfort
  4. Create fasteners as with cloth pads

Wool pads are more durable than cloth (5-7 years) and require less frequent washing due to wool's natural antimicrobial properties.

Natural Skin Care Products

Cold Cream Base

An excellent moisturizer and protective barrier:

  1. Combine 4 tablespoons animal fat or coconut oil with 1 tablespoon beeswax
  2. Heat gently over water bath until both ingredients melt
  3. Remove from heat and add 2 tablespoons rosewater or herbal infusion (cooled)
  4. Whisk constantly as mixture cools until it reaches light, creamy texture
  5. Store in sealed clay pot

Healing Salve (For Wounds, Burns, Inflammation)

Ingredients:

IngredientQuantityProperties
Lard or rendered animal fat4 ouncesProtective base
Beeswax1 ounceStiffens salve, protective
Dried yarrow2 tablespoonsHemostatic, promotes healing
Dried plantain leaves2 tablespoonsDrawing agent, infection prevention
Dried calendula (marigold)1 tablespoonSkin regeneration, antimicrobial
Essential oil (lavender)3-5 dropsAntimicrobial preservation

Preparation:

  1. Heat fat gently until nearly melted
  2. Add herbs, remove from heat, and allow to infuse 30 minutes
  3. Strain through cloth, pressing all liquid from herbs
  4. Return strained liquid to heat, add beeswax
  5. Once beeswax dissolves, add essential oil and stir
  6. Pour into small pots to cool
  7. Solidifies in 1-2 hours; store in cool location

Herbal Infusion for Face Washing

Simple, effective cleansing without soap:

  1. Choose herb(s): rose petals for sensitive skin, calendula for troubled skin, chamomile for all types
  2. Steep 1 handful dried herb in 1 cup hot water 15-30 minutes
  3. Strain and cool
  4. Use as daily wash with soft cloth, or store in sealed vessel for up to 1 week

Lip Balm

Essential for exposed faces in harsh conditions:

  1. Combine 1 tablespoon beeswax with 1 tablespoon animal fat or coconut oil
  2. Heat gently until melted
  3. Add 1-2 drops peppermint or rose essential oil
  4. Pour into small containers (small bark tubes work well)
  5. Cool completely before use
