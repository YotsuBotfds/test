Smoked paper

Drum seismographs often wrote on smoked paper, blackened over a sooty flame. A fine stylus on the seismometer's lever scratched the soot away. The drum turned once per line while the stylus crept sideways, so a day became one long spiral, later fixed with varnish.

A clock kicked the stylus every minute, leaving the small ticks along each line. Ember marks are the catalogue's origin times, so the gap to the first wiggle is the travel time.

    A synthetic record, not a recording. Arrival times are ray-traced through the iasp91 Earth model (Kennett and Engdahl, 1991), giving P and S out to the core's shadow, diffracted waves inside it, and PKP or SKS beyond. Body waves weaken with distance roughly as Δ−1.5; surface waves follow the Prague formula used for surface-wave magnitude (Δ−1.66), fade for sources deeper than 30 km, and circle the planet up to four times. Rupture duration grows √10 times per magnitude unit. The ocean microseism, its storms and seasons are modelled. Station coordinates are from FDSN metadata for the IU network. Sound is the same seismogram sped up, band-limited and gently compressed.
