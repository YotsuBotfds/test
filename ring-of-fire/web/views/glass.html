02 Interior

Glass Earth

Here the planet is made of glass and every quake hangs at its own depth. Where one plate dives under another, the quakes follow it down into the mantle as a glowing curtain hundreds of kilometres deep. Below them all sits the molten core.

    Why the quakes go deep

    Where an ocean plate sinks under another, it scrapes and bends on the way down, and the quakes trace its upper part as a sloping sheet. Seismologists call these sheets Wadati–Benioff zones, after Kiyoo Wadati and Hugo Benioff, who mapped them between the 1920s and 1950s.

    Below about 70 km the mantle is hot enough to flow, so deep quakes happen only inside the sinking slab, which stays cold for millions of years.
