04 Waves

Through the Planet

A large quake sends two kinds of waves through the body of the planet. P waves push and pull the rock and travel fastest. S waves shake it side to side at a little over half that speed, and cannot cross a liquid. Follow both from a real quake, through the mantle and the core, to cities around the world. The places they cannot reach reveal what lies inside.

    What the shadows told us

    In 1906 Richard Oldham noticed that waves crossing deep through the planet arrive later than they should, and argued for a central core that slows them. In 1914 Beno Gutenberg placed its edge 2,900 km down, from the shadow it casts on the far side.

    S waves need rigid rock to travel. They never cross the core, and in 1926 Harold Jeffreys argued that the core is liquid. In 1936 Inge Lehmann explained faint P waves that arrive inside the shadow: an inner core where P waves travel faster bends them there. Press play and watch for them.
